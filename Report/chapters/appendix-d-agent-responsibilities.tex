\chapter{Detailed Agent Responsibility Views}
\label{app:agent-responsibilities}

The main architecture chapter keeps the agent design compact. This appendix
keeps the additional responsibility views that are useful for technical review
but would make the main chapter too heavy. Each view follows the same pattern:
input context, agent responsibility, output contract, and guardrails.

\section{Orchestrator}

The orchestrator owns the route graph. It does not inspect websites directly.
Its value is control: it builds handoffs, keeps state, aggregates evidence, and
decides whether downstream provider and email steps should run.

\section{Classification Agent}

The classification agent has the smallest browser budget because its task is
narrow. It must identify the page role, not solve the whole investigation. This
keeps the first decision fast and prevents the system from wasting browser
actions on the wrong page type.

\input{figures/tikz/fig-04-agent-card-classification}

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Q{3.5cm}Y}
\toprule
\rowcolor{accent!8}
Responsibility & Detail \\
\midrule
Signals used & Title, visible text, links, iframes, player hints, screenshots,
and page structure. \\
Output & \texttt{ClassificationResult} with page type, confidence, and route
reason. \\
Failure mode & Mixed-role pages can contain both listing and player signals. \\
Guardrail & The classifier does not click, navigate, or harvest streams. \\
\bottomrule

\section{Landing-Page Agent}

The landing-page agent is responsible for discovery. It should not try to
activate players unless the page clearly becomes a hosting page. Its job is to
inspect event cards, tabs, schedules, and links, then pass good candidates to
the next stage.

\input{figures/tikz/fig-04-agent-card-landing}

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Q{3.5cm}Y}
\toprule
\rowcolor{accent!8}
Responsibility & Detail \\
\midrule
Evidence target & Match cards, event links, channel pages, watch buttons, host
candidates, and visible broadcaster hints. \\
Interaction policy & Scroll, open date tabs, reveal hidden sections, and avoid
unrelated navigation. \\
Output & Candidate URLs ranked with match or page context. \\
Guardrail & Stop after visible discovery is covered; do not crawl the entire
domain. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Landing-agent responsibility checklist.}
\label{tab:appendix-landing-checklist}
\end{table}

\section{Hosting-Page Agent}

The hosting-page agent performs the most fragile browser work. It must activate
server buttons and player controls while handling popups and redirects. It also
decides when a page should be handed to the embedded agent.

\input{figures/tikz/fig-04-agent-card-hosting}

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Q{3.5cm}Y}
\toprule
\rowcolor{accent!8}
Responsibility & Detail \\
\midrule
Evidence target & Player activation, stream candidates, iframe URLs, server
attempts, and screenshots. \\
Interaction policy & Try server buttons deliberately, close popups, restore
focus, and verify player state before harvesting. \\
Output & Streams when available, otherwise embedded-player handoff or clear
failure reason. \\
Guardrail & Do not mark success from a screenshot alone; require stream,
embedded, or blocker evidence. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Hosting-agent responsibility checklist.}

\section{Embedded-Page Agent}

The embedded-page agent works on direct player or iframe URLs. It is often the
fallback when a hosting page exposes an iframe but does not return media
evidence at the host level.

\toprule
\rowcolor{accent!8}
Responsibility & Detail \\
\midrule
Evidence target & Player runtime, nested frame URLs, manifests, media requests,
and visual playback state. \\
Interaction policy & Inspect first, click play only when needed, wait for
network activity, then harvest. \\
Output & Stream evidence, player type, screenshots, or reason why playback was

Guardrail & Do not crawl away from the assigned embedded/player context. \\

\section{Provider and Email Stage}

Provider analysis and email drafting run only after stream or embedded evidence
exists. They are downstream packaging stages, not browser investigators. This
separation avoids mixing technical extraction with review communication.

\input{figures/tikz/fig-04-agent-card-provider-email}

\toprule
\rowcolor{accent!8}
Responsibility & Detail \\
\midrule
Input & Stream URLs, source pages, screenshots, provider lookups, and rights
context. \\
Output & Provider rows and email drafts that remain subject to human approval. \\
Failure mode & Provider can be known while abuse contact is missing. \\
Guardrail & Do not run when no stream or embedded evidence exists, and do not
invent missing provider details. \\

\section{Why These Boundaries Matter}

The boundaries were introduced because early experiments mixed too many
responsibilities. A single broad agent could classify a page, click a link,
open a popup, harvest too early, and still produce a confident final message.
That made failures difficult to understand. Splitting the runtime into bounded
agents made each failure more concrete:

\begin{itemize}
  \item wrong page type means classification or handoff issue;
  \item no candidate links means landing extraction issue;
  \item no player activation means hosting interaction issue;
  \item no manifest after iframe handoff means embedded extraction issue;
  \item provider found but no abuse contact means enrichment coverage issue;
  \item high cost with no new evidence means tool-budget or prompt issue.
\end{itemize}

This responsibility model also supports future work. New specialists can be
added without rewriting the whole pipeline, and regression tests can target one
stage at a time.
