\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Host organization -- Soft Stars}

Soft Stars is a software company working in digital media intelligence, content
protection, and technical support for rights-enforcement operations. Its work
focuses on helping media organizations observe online distribution channels,
identify unauthorized use of protected content, and prepare technical evidence
that can be reviewed by operational, legal, or business teams.

The internship was carried out within Soft Stars in the context of sports media
protection. The project was connected to the broader problem of monitoring
illegal streaming websites and improving the way evidence is collected from
them. At this stage of the report, the system is introduced only from a general
business and engineering perspective. The detailed process, implementation
choices, and evaluation are developed progressively in the following chapters.

\section{Sports piracy and the evidence collection challenge}

Premium sports broadcasting depends on exclusive distribution rights. Broadcasters
pay for the right to transmit competitions in specific territories, on specific
platforms, and under controlled commercial conditions. Illegal streaming websites
undermine this model by redistributing protected events without authorization,
often through domains that change frequently and through pages designed to be
difficult to monitor manually.

The first practical challenge in this context is not only finding suspicious
websites. It is collecting evidence that is precise, traceable, and useful. A
rights holder needs to know which page was reachable, when it was observed, what
content or player it exposed, and which technical indicators can support a later
review or enforcement action. Screenshots, visible page state, embedded players,
network indicators, and stream references can all become important, but they are
often hidden behind dynamic interfaces, popups, nested frames, language variation,
and short-lived links.

This makes evidence collection an engineering problem. Manual review is slow and
inconsistent when the number of targets grows. Simple scraping is too fragile for
modern streaming pages, because the useful evidence may appear only after the
page loads scripts, reacts to user actions, or redirects through several layers.
The general goal is therefore to support a more reliable way of observing these
websites, collecting evidence, and keeping enough context for the result to be
auditable.

\section{Objectives and contributions}

The objective of this project is to study and design a technical approach for
evidence collection from illegal sports streaming websites. The work aims to
move from a manual and case-by-case process toward a more structured process
that can inspect target pages, preserve useful observations, and present results
in a form that can be reviewed.

The main objectives are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item understand the operational context of sports piracy monitoring and the
    type of evidence required by rights-protection teams;
  \item analyze the technical behavior of illegal streaming websites, including
    dynamic pages, embedded players, redirects, and multilingual layouts;
  \item define a maintainable evidence-collection process that can adapt to
    different website structures;
  \item design a system architecture that separates observation, extraction,
    persistence, review, and evaluation concerns;
  \item evaluate the approach on representative targets and document its limits.
\end{itemize}

The main contributions of the internship are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a structured analysis of the evidence-collection problem in the sports
    piracy domain;
  \item a progressive design of an automated collection approach, from general
    requirements to implementation details;
  \item a technical architecture for collecting, storing, and reviewing evidence
    from suspicious streaming websites;
  \item an evaluation of the approach based on observed behavior, collected
    traces, and failure cases;
  \item a report structure that moves from the general business problem toward
    the specialized implementation and validation work.
\end{itemize}

\section{Report structure}

This report is organized from general context to specialized implementation.
Chapter~2 presents the methodology, requirements analysis, and design constraints
used to frame the project. Chapter~3 introduces the technical background needed
to understand streaming websites, browser-based observation, automation tools,
agentic extraction, and media protocols. Chapter~4 describes the proposed
architecture and explains how the main system responsibilities are organized.
Chapter~5 details the implementation choices and the concrete mechanisms used to
collect and structure evidence. Chapter~6 evaluates the results, discusses
observed limitations, and analyzes the reliability of the approach. Chapter~7
concludes the report.
